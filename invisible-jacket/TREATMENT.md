# The Invisible Jacket: treatment (version 2)

Style: Crayon Picture Book (`styles/crayon-book/STYLE.md`). About 56 s, 1920×1080, 24 fps. English narration (the language chosen at the start), burned-in subtitles plus `.srt`. Loudness −14 LUFS.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **A walk home that takes a detour (chosen).** One long page, two tiers: the street at the bottom, the neighbour's garden above and behind the fence (child-logic perspective). The camera follows Leo along the street, then tilts up into the garden. The page is used top to bottom, not just as a strip.
2. **Before and after.** The garden seen twice: first while Leo is invisible (outlines only, no colour), then as a guest (coloured in). Strong idea, but it spends half the film on the same place.
3. **A list that turns.** "Invisible, he could see…": a series of secrets in the neighbourhood, one per beat, ending at the lonely garden. More gags, but no single character to care about.

Structure 1 keeps one hero and one goal. I take one idea from structure 2: Leo becomes coloured in again when he stops hiding.

**Opening, ending and score, chosen from the topic:**
- Opening: Leo is drawn mid-stride on the first beat, already walking; the title writes itself in the sky beside him.
- Ending: a cannonball splash. A crayon column of water shoots up out of the pool and bursts into an ultramarine crown in the sky, where the wash finds white-wax words: "The End". The pool, pale until now, turns deep blue.
- Score shape: a bouncy ukulele walk, a sneaky bassoon while he is invisible, a music-box tune for the garden, two real silences, then everyone together on the splash.

## 2. Logline and arc

**Logline:** A boy walking home finds a jacket that makes him invisible, sneaks into the garden next door, and finds a lonely neighbour beside a pool nobody swims in. He gives up being invisible to say hello.

- **Setup:** Leo walks home the same way as always and kicks a stone into the hedge. It clinks against something.
- **Turn:** the jacket makes him invisible. He sneaks into the garden and finds the lady alone. He doesn't want to hide any more and takes the jacket off.
- **Ending:** she invites him to swim. His splash floods the page, and the pool is never empty again.

## 3. Native moves

- **Invisible means paper (signature shot).** Wearing the jacket, Leo is drawn as a knock-out: a boy-shaped hole of bare paper. Against the sky he vanishes completely; against the fence, hedge and lawn you see a white silhouette walking. Only this medium can do it.
- **Wax resist, twice.** The jacket is white crayon in the dark of the hedge: invisible until the wash fills the hollow. "The End" is white crayon in the garden sky, found by the splash. (Laid down at the splash: drawn from frame one, the white letters showed faintly on the paper and spoiled the surprise.)
- **Colouring is an action.** When Leo takes the jacket off, he is coloured back in, row by row.
- **Drawn live.** The garden draws and colours itself as the camera tilts up to it.
- **Boil.** Calms during the lady's sad moment.

## 4. Benchmark

- *Lost and Found* (Oliver Jeffers): learn how two lonely characters and a small gesture carry a story with very few words. Don't take: characters, designs, text, the penguin.
- *I Want My Hat Back* (Jon Klassen): learn deadpan timing, and letting the viewer see what a character can't (the rabbit sniffing the invisible boy). Don't take: designs, animals, typography.

## 5. Characters

- **Leo:** about seven. Round head, short spiky brown hair, orange T-shirt, navy shorts, white socks, red trainers, a yellow school backpack. Outline in indigo.
- **The lady next door:** older, taller, silver-lilac hair in a bun, round glasses, a straw sun hat with a red band, a sky-blue dress. Sits in a red-striped deckchair.
- **Supporting:** a brown rabbit, a tortoise, and a yellow rubber duck alone in the pool.

- **2.0 s:** "Every day, Leo walked home the same way."
- **7.2 s:** the kick. **8.2 s:** clink.

- **9.5–10.9 s:** silence 1 (crickets only).
- **10.9–12.3 s:** the wash sweeps the hollow; **12.3 s:** glockenspiel as the jacket appears.
- **12.9 s:** Leo, surprised. **13.2 s:** "A jacket, as white as nothing at all."
- **13.6 / 14.3 s:** arms into sleeves (two drawings). **15.0 s:** zip. **16.4 s:** pop, invisible.
- **17.0 s:** "And Leo was gone." He waves his invisible hands.
- **19.1–25.2 s:** sneaking along the fence, bassoon staccato and woodblock on his steps.
- **20.0 s:** "Invisible, he could go anywhere."
- **25.2 s:** the gate creaks; garden sounds start early (J-cut).
- **27.3 s:** tilt up; the music-box tune starts; the garden draws itself.
- **30.4 s:** "Next door was a pool that nobody ever swam in,"
- **33.6 s:** "and a lady who sat beside it, all alone."
- **35.5 s:** the rabbit hops over and sniffs the invisible boy; he freezes.
- **38.2 s:** her sigh. **38.9–40.9 s:** silence 2.

## 10. Subtitles and titles

- Subtitles: Patrick Hand 54 px, indigo, bottom centre on a ragged knock-out patch of paper, written left to right in 0.35 s; hold ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). None during wordless passages.
- Title: "The Invisible Jacket" in Gaegu Bold, written letter by letter in the street sky. It leaves when the camera moves off it.
- "Hello!": hand-lettered speech bubble beside Leo.
- "The End": white wax in the garden sky from frame one, revealed by the flood.

## 11. Content note

The jacket lets Leo watch someone without being seen. The story treats that as the thing he gives up: he takes the jacket off, says hello, and is invited in.

## 12. Changes made during review

- A full-page flood made the whole picture muddy grey-blue (every crayon area resists the wash, only the paper takes it). Replaced by the column of water and the sky crown, which keep the lawn green and the frame bright.
- The pool is coloured lightly, so the wash can deepen it: a pale, still pool becomes deep blue when someone finally swims in it.
- White-wax bubbles over the lawn were visible before the splash (white crayon shows on top of colour); bubbles are now drawn with the splash.
- The signpost moved left so it doesn't hide Leo in the opening; the lady and her chair appear once the lawn is drawn.
- The "Hello!" bubble holds 2.2 s (minimum reading time 1.9 s).

## 13. Not the demo again

Compared with `styles/crayon-book/DEMO.md`: opening (a boy already walking, vs a blank page and a moon drawn on), signature shot (the invisible paper-hole boy, vs the wash revealing stars), camera path (street truck, tilt up into a second tier, vs a one-page climb), score (4/4 walk / sneak / music box / tutti, vs a 3/4 lullaby) and ending (splash crown, vs a pull-back to a book and page turn) all differ. The structure (one continuous page) is shared.
